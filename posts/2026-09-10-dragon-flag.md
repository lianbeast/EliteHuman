The dragon flag is the first exercise I ever failed at on purpose.

2015. I'd seen it in a Bruce Lee documentary — the whole body rigid as
a pole, lowering from the shoulders, core doing everything, momentum
forbidden. I lay down on a bench, gripped the edge behind my head, and
discovered that my body had opinions about this plan. All of them were
no.

## The progression nobody films

What the highlight reels skip: it took a year to own the movement.

- **Hip-ups first** — bend at the hips, come back up. The cheat version,
  done honestly.
- **Negatives next** — lower slowly, reset by climbing down. Every rep
  a small defeat you choose.
- **Partial range** — lower to forty-five degrees, hold, scream
  internally, return.
- **Full flag** — one day it just happens, and it feels like the bench
  owes you money.

## What it taught me

The dragon flag is a patience test wearing a strength test's clothes.
You cannot rush it without your lower back filing a formal complaint —
and it wins that argument every time.

Ten years on, I still do them. Fewer reps, more respect. Bruce was
right about the exercise. He was more right about the *doing*.
