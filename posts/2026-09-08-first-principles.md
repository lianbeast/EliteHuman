I used to think the program was the secret. Find the right split, the
right percentages, the right exercise order — and the body would have
no choice but to grow.

Nine years of marks later, I think it's simpler and harder than that.

## The program is the smallest part

Every good program I've run shared exactly three traits:

1. It was hard enough to force adaptation.
2. It was simple enough that I never wondered what came next.
3. It was mine — I chose it, so I defended it on the days I didn't feel like it.

Everything else was decoration. Sets of five or sets of eight, barbell
or dumbbell, push or pull — the body adapts to load and consistency. It
does not read spreadsheets.

## Ordinary isn't gonna cut it

The archive you can browse from this site is 105 marks. Not 105 perfect
sessions — 105 *kept*. Rainy days, deadlifts that felt like concrete,
mornings where the win was showing up at all.

That's the actual first principle: **the mark only exists if it's
recorded.** A training session you don't count is a vote for the
ordinary life.

## What this blog is for

Longer thoughts than an Instagram caption allows: training,
discipline, the philosophy that keeps the streak alive. The marks stay
on the feed. The reasoning lives here.
